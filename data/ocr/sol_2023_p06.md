所以 $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_{2n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left\{ -\frac{2}{4n^2\pi^2} \left[ (-1)^{2n} - 1 \right] \right\} = 0$.

$\square$

14. 设连续函数 $f(x)$ 满足：$f(x+2) - f(x) = x$, $\int_0^2 f(x)\mathrm{d}x = 0$, 则 $\int_1^3 f(x)\mathrm{d}x = \underline{\quad\frac{1}{2}\quad}$.

解. 由 $f(x+2) - f(x) = x$ 得：$\int_0^1 f(x+2)\mathrm{d}x - \int_0^1 f(x)\mathrm{d}x = \int_0^1 x\mathrm{d}x$, 又
$$
\int_0^1 f(x+2)\mathrm{d}x \xrightarrow{x+2=u} \int_2^3 f(u)\mathrm{d}u = \int_2^3 f(x)\mathrm{d}x, \int_0^1 x\mathrm{d}x = \frac{1}{2}
$$
所以有：
$$
\int_2^3 f(x)\mathrm{d}x - \int_0^1 f(x)\mathrm{d}x = \int_1^3 f(x)\mathrm{d}x - \int_1^2 f(x)\mathrm{d}x - \int_0^1 f(x)\mathrm{d}x = \int_1^3 f(x)\mathrm{d}x - \int_0^2 f(x)\mathrm{d}x = \frac{1}{2}
$$
又 $\int_0^2 f(x)\mathrm{d}x = 0$, 所以 $\int_1^3 f(x)\mathrm{d}x = \frac{1}{2}$.

$\square$

15. 已知向量 $\alpha_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}, \alpha_2 = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ -1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}, \alpha_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}, \beta = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}, \gamma = k_1\alpha_1 + k_2\alpha_2 + k_3\alpha_3$. 若 $\gamma^T\alpha_i = \beta^T\alpha_i (i = 1,2,3)$, 则 $k_1^2 + k_2^2 + k_3^2 = \underline{\quad\frac{11}{9}\quad}$.

解. 注意到：$\alpha_i^T\alpha_i = 3 (i = 1,2,3), \alpha_1^T\alpha_2 = \alpha_1^T\alpha_3 = \alpha_2^T\alpha_3 = 0$, 于是有：
$$
\gamma^T\alpha_i = (k_1\alpha_1 + k_2\alpha_2 + k_3\alpha_3)^T\alpha_i = k_1\alpha_1^T\alpha_i + k_2\alpha_2^T\alpha_i + k_3\alpha_3^T\alpha_i = k_i\alpha_i^T\alpha_i = 3k_i (i = 1,2,3),
$$
则有：
$$
3k_1 = \beta^T\alpha_1 = 1,\ 3k_2 = \beta^T\alpha_2 = -3,\ 3k_3 = \beta^T\alpha_3 = -1,
$$
从而有：$k_1 = \frac{1}{3},\ k_2 = -1,\ k_3 = -\frac{1}{3}$, 故：$k_1^2 + k_2^2 + k_3^2 = \frac{11}{9}$.

$\square$

16. 设随机变量 $X$ 与 $Y$ 相互独立，且 $X \sim B\left(1, \frac{1}{3}\right), Y \sim B\left(2, \frac{1}{2}\right)$, 则 $P\{X = Y\} = \underline{\quad\frac{1}{3}\quad}$.

解.
$$
\begin{aligned}
P\{X = Y\} &= P\{X = 0, Y = 0\} + P\{X = 1, Y = 1\} \\
&= P\{X = 0\}P\{Y = 0\} + P\{X = 1\}P\{Y = 1\} \\
&= \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$\square$
